6.6220 is the graduate level power electronics class at MIT taught by Dave Perrault. It's a class I very much enjoyed taking and is an overview of many topics in power electronics. As a final design project for the class we has to make a design for relatively simple mid-range power buck converter. We had to do all the design work including component selection, filter design, magnetics design, thermal analysis, and controller design. Due to time constraint we were not required to physically build this circuit or any PCB design. We did have to prove our design fulfilled certain efficiency, ripple, and transient requirements through reasonable analysis and simulation. 

The design I came up with appears to work well according to my analysis. My design is not particularly special in any way, but the processes taught me a lot about how to go about designing my own power converters, particularly in the importance of considering design aspects outside the main power switching cell and singlehandedly following efficiency. A full write-up of my design is linked [here]({{ site.baseurl }}/assets/2025-05-12-6622-design-project-files/6_622_Design_Project.pdf).
